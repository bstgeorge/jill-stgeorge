NASPAA Standard 5.1

The Five Core Competencies

The universal required competencies that form the foundation of every accredited Master of Public Administration program.

I. Competency One

To lead and manage in the public interest.

Public sector leadership requires balancing competing priorities — public safety, fiscal responsibility, equity, and community identity — while operating within the constraints of limited funding and complex intergovernmental relationships. Effective leaders move beyond identifying problems to evaluate options grounded in evidence and the public good.

II. Competency Two

To participate in, and contribute to, the public policy process.

Contributing to public policy is not limited to elected officials. It includes anyone who gathers evidence, elevates community voices, and translates findings into clear, actionable recommendations. Politically neutral administrators bring practical insight, transparency, and rigor that strengthen policy creation at every level of government.

Featured Work

III. Competency Three

To analyze, synthesize, think critically, solve problems, and make evidence-informed decisions in a complex and dynamic environment.

This competency sits at the heart of professional public service — the ability to move beyond identifying a problem and work through it with confidence. In a field where administrations change, public opinion shifts, and resources are constrained, public administrators must analyze complex information, weigh competing values, and arrive at sound, defensible recommendations.

IV. Competency Four

To articulate, apply, and advance a public service perspective.

Operating at the intersection of policy and the public, administrators translate the directives of elected officials into action. Unlike private sector employees, government workers cannot rely on market feedback to gauge effectiveness. A strong public service perspective sustains efficiency and accountability in the face of public scrutiny and long-horizon outcomes.

Featured Work

V. Competency Five

To communicate and interact productively and in culturally responsive ways with a diverse and changing workforce and society at large.

Communities are becoming increasingly diverse, and navigating cultural differences competently and sensitively is essential to public service. Government that reflects the composition of the public it serves is more likely to be perceived as fair and effective. Effective communication determines who actually benefits from public programs.
